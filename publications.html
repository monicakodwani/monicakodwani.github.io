How Probing for Problems and Bias Affects Perceptions of AI Chatbot Trustworthiness

Jan Tolsdorf, Alan F. Luo, Monica Kodwani, Junho Eum, Mahmood Sharif, Michelle L. Mazurek, Adam J. Aviv

ACM Conference on Fairness, Accountability, and Transparency

Interactive study with 254 U.S. participants who held 551 open-ended conversations while investigating problematic, questionable, or unfair chatbot responses. Participants frequently failed to uncover the issues they expected, trustworthiness perceptions generally increased after probing, and participants with higher initial trust were less likely to flag problems.

“Because I Didn’t Touch These and Even Don’t Know Why I Should Change These”: Why App Developers Do (Not) Update Apple’s Privacy Labels

Arwa Alsahdi, Jialiang Yan, Monica Kodwani, Matthias Fassl, Chris Kanich, Adam J. Aviv

Proceedings on Privacy Enhancing Technologies, 2026(4), 906–926

A four-year analysis of privacy-label changes across 926,240 iOS apps, complemented by responses from 128 developers and interviews with 19 developers. Fewer than 6% of apps changed their labels. The study shows how uncertainty about third-party data practices, fragmented responsibility, and limited workflow support can encourage minimal or optimistic disclosure.

ARIA Evaluation Planning Manual: Elements of ARIA-Style AI Evaluations

Theodore Jensen, Mark Przybocki, Monica Kodwani, Kristen Greene, Patrick Hall, Razvan Amironesei, Craig Greenberg

NIST Trustworthy and Responsible AI report (NIST AI 200-3)

A planning manual for NIST's Assessing Risks and Impacts of AI (ARIA) evaluations, which assess an AI application's trustworthiness by combining three kinds of testing: model testing, red teaming, and user testing. The manual outlines the elements each evaluation needs, from study materials to supporting infrastructure.

Safety Perceptions of Generative AI Conversational Agents: Uncovering Perceptual Differences in Trust, Risk, and Fairness

Jan Tolsdorf, Alan F. Luo, Monica Kodwani, Junho Eum, Mahmood Sharif, Michelle L. Mazurek, Adam J. Aviv

Symposium on Usable Privacy and Security

Survey of 123 U.S. chatbot users examining perceived trust, fairness, and risk. Factor and latent-class analyses identified three groups — Hesitant Skeptics, Cautious Trusters, and Confident Adopters — and found that more frequent chatbot use was associated with higher trust and fairness perceptions and lower perceived risk.

On a Scale of 1 to 5, How Reliable Are AI User Studies? A Call for Developing Validated, Meaningful Scales and Metrics about User Perceptions of AI Systems

Jan Tolsdorf, Alan F. Luo, Monica Kodwani, Junho Eum, Mahmood Sharif, Michelle L. Mazurek, Adam J. Aviv

IEEE Security & Privacy Workshops — Workshop on Technology and Consumer Protection

Review of 130 AI user studies examining how researchers measure perceptions such as trust and fairness. Most studies relied on unvalidated rating items, motivating a call for validated instruments, clearer construct definitions, and shared measurement resources.

Mir Masood Ali, David G. Balash, Monica Kodwani, Chris Kanich, Adam J. Aviv

Proceedings on Privacy Enhancing Technologies

Compared Apple privacy labels with the corresponding apps' privacy policies using language-model-assisted analysis, identifying discrepancies between the practices described in the two forms of disclosure.

Longitudinal Analysis of Privacy Labels in the Apple App Store

David G. Balash, Mir Masood Ali, Monica Kodwani, Xiaoyuan Wu, Chris Kanich, Adam J. Aviv

arXiv preprint · Preprint

Weekly snapshots of 1.6 million apps (July 2021 – October 2022) tracking privacy-label adoption over time. Found roughly 70% adoption, with growth coming mostly from newly listed apps and labels rarely updated after creation — raising questions about their reliability.
